# History of Humanoid Robotics

Humanoid robotics has progressed through four waves:

## **1. Early Mechanical Automatons (1700s–1900s)**
Early clockwork “robots” mimicked simple human motions—writing automata, mechanical musicians.

No autonomy; purely mechanical.

## **2. Electromechanical Humanoids (1920–1970)**
Robots gained motors, sensors, and electrical control.

Key milestones:
- 1927: *Televox* — one of the first voice-controlled machines  
- 1948: *Elsie* — early mobile autonomous robot  
- 1960s: Biped walking research expands in Japan  

## **3. The Age of Programmed Humanoids (1980–2010)**
Humanoids gain microprocessors and complex kinematics.

Notable systems:
- Honda P2, P3 → leading to **ASIMO**  
- Sony **QRIO**  
- KAIST **HUBO**  
- Boston Dynamics early bipeds  

Robots could walk, run, climb stairs—but movements were scripted using deterministic control.

## **4. Embodied AI Era (2015–Today)**
The revolution enabling this course:

- Deep learning for perception  
- GPU-accelerated simulation  
- VLA and LLM-driven planning  
- Real-time SLAM on embedded hardware  
- Affordable quadrupeds and small humanoids  
- Commercial humanoids (Unitree G1, Tesla Optimus)  

Today, robots can be trained in simulation and deployed in reality with unprecedented capability.

Your work sits in this fourth wave.
